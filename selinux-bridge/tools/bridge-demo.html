Press Start camera. Every frame has the time it was captured burned into its top-left corner — compare that with real time now.

Chromium plays this file from its first byte and never seeks, so the picture starts as far behind the present as the file was long when the camera opened, and that offset never closes. The page now truncates the feed before opening, which is what keeps picture is near zero instead of minutes. Press Re-open camera to reset it again and watch the number drop back.

This meter is Chromium's synthetic test tone, not your phone. The switch that lets this page inject the phone camera (--use-fake-device-for-media-stream) replaces the microphone at the same time — one switch, both devices. A tab can have the real camera or the real microphone, never both. For genuinely live phone audio, run bridge-mic and open a browser without that switch; the level below then follows the room.

Clap once, sharply, about two seconds in. The clip captures the phone camera and the phone microphone through the same MediaRecorder, so both land on one timeline. The clap is visible (hands meeting) and audible (a transient) in the same file, and the distance between those two marks is the audio/video skew — measured without needing any outside reference.
